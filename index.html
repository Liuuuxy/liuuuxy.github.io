Hi! I am a computer science PhD student at Arizona State University, advised by Prof. Ransalu Senanayake and affiliated with the Laboratory for Learning Evaluation and Naturalization of Systems (LENS Lab).

I work on information-efficient AI systems — how intelligent systems can make better decisions from limited but informative signals. In practice that takes a few forms: choosing and routing among large language models from a handful of user interactions; evaluating humanoid robots against cognitive and human-factors criteria rather than task completion alone; letting an LLM plan for a robot crew while a procedural layer keeps the authority to act; and learning embodied skills from small numbers of demonstrations.

Before ASU I was a research assistant in the MAPLE Lab at Vanderbilt University, working with Prof. Jie Ying Wu and Prof. Nabil Simaan on multimodal models for surgical robotics, and before that a machine learning intern at Ra Labs. I received my M.S. in Computer Science from Rice University and my B.S. in Software Engineering from Huazhong University of Science & Technology.

I’m always happy to connect about research, collaborate on ideas, or share advice. Please feel free to get in touch!

  2. CUPID in the Model Zoo: Online Matchmaking for Selecting Your Dream LLM

    Son Nguyen, Xinyuan Liu, Ransalu Senanayake

  3. Humanoid Factors: Design Principles for AI Humanoids in Human Worlds

    Xinyuan Liu, Eren Sadikoglu, Ransalu Senanayake, Lixiao Huang
    arXiv preprint arXiv:2602.10069, 2026 · Abridged version, “Cognitive Models as Evaluation Primitives in Humanoid Factors,” at the ICLR 2026 Workshop on From Human Cognition to AI Reasoning
    Human factors research optimizes environments for people; humanoids sharing those environments need factors of their own. We propose humanoid factors — physical, cognitive, social, and ethical pillars — and apply the framework to a real humanoid control algorithm, showing what conventional task-completion metrics in robotics leave out.
