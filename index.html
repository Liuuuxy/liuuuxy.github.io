PhD Student, Computer Science · Arizona State University · LENS Lab

Hi! I'm Xinyuan Liu, a second-year Computer Science PhD student at Arizona State University, advised by Prof. Ransalu Senanayake and affiliated with the LENS Lab.

I work on information-efficient AI systems: how intelligent systems can make better decisions from limited but informative signals. That question runs through choosing and routing among large language models from a handful of user interactions, evaluating humanoid robots against cognitive and human-factors criteria rather than task completion alone, letting an LLM plan for a crew of robots while a procedural layer keeps the authority to act, and learning embodied skills from few demonstrations.

Previously, I was a research assistant in the MAPLE Lab at Vanderbilt University, working with Prof. Jie Ying Wu on multimodal models for surgical robotics, and a machine learning intern at Ra Labs. I received my M.S. in Computer Science from Rice University and my B.S. in Software Engineering from Huazhong University of Science and Technology.

I'm always happy to connect about research, collaborate on ideas, or share advice. Please feel free to get in touch!

  6. Cognitive Models as Evaluation Primitives in Humanoid Factors

    Xinyuan Liu, Eren Sadikoglu, Ransalu Senanayake, Lixiao Huang
    arXiv preprint, 2026
    Abridged version at the ICLR 2026 Workshop on From Human Cognition to AI Reasoning.
    Human factors research optimizes environments for people; humanoids that share those environments need factors of their own. We propose humanoid factors — physical, cognitive, social and ethical pillars — and apply the framework to a real humanoid control algorithm, showing what conventional task-completion metrics in robotics leave out.
